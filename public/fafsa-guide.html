FAFSA Guide

Learn what the FAFSA is, what you need to complete it, and how filing early can help you access financial aid for college.

What Is FAFSA?

Free Application for Federal Student Aid

The FAFSA is the application students use to apply for federal financial aid for college or career school. Many states and colleges also use information from the FAFSA when determining eligibility for their own financial aid. Completing the FAFSA can help you find grants, scholarships, work-study opportunities, and student loans.

Before You Start

01. Create Your FSA ID

Students generally need a StudentAid.gov account to access and complete the FAFSA. If a parent or contributor needs to provide information, they may need their own account as well.

02. Gather Your Information

Have the information needed to complete the application available before you begin. This may include tax information, records of assets, Social Security information, and details about the colleges you are considering.

03. Know Your Colleges

Add the colleges or career schools you are considering to your FAFSA so they can receive your FAFSA information and use it when determining your financial aid eligibility.

04. Understand Contributors

Depending on your circumstances, a parent or another contributor may need to provide information on the FAFSA. Make sure everyone who needs to participate understands their role before you begin.

Completing the FAFSA

01. Complete Every Section

Carefully work through the FAFSA and answer each question that applies to you. Read the instructions closely and double-check your answers before moving forward.

FAFSA Step-By-Step

02. Review Your Information

Before submitting, review names, dates, financial information, school selections, and other important details. Mistakes can create delays or require corrections.

03. Submit Your FAFSA

Complete the required signatures and submit your FAFSA. Save confirmation information and keep copies of important records for your files.

04. Watch for Follow-Up

Check your StudentAid.gov account and email for updates. Your college may also contact you if additional documents or information are needed before your financial aid can be finalized.

FAFSA Checklist

StudentAid.gov account: Make sure the student has the necessary account credentials.
Parent or contributor information: Make sure required contributors are prepared to participate.
Tax information: Have the financial information needed for the application available.
College list: Know which schools should receive your FAFSA information.
Important records: Keep copies of confirmation numbers and other FAFSA records.
Deadlines: Check federal, state, and college-specific financial aid deadlines.

After You Submit

01. Review Your FAFSA Information

Keep an eye on your StudentAid.gov account for updates, notifications, and information about the status of your FAFSA.

02. Check Your College Emails

Colleges may contact you with requests for additional information or documents. Respond as quickly as possible when something is requested.

03. Compare Financial Aid Offers

Once colleges send financial aid information, carefully compare grants, scholarships, work-study, and loans to understand what each school is offering.

04. Ask Questions

If you do not understand your financial aid package, contact the school's financial aid office. Asking questions early can help you make a more informed college decision.

Don't Wait Until the Last Minute

Financial aid deadlines can vary by federal, state, and college programs. Completing your FAFSA as early as practical can help you stay ahead of deadlines and give colleges the information they need to determine your financial aid eligibility.
